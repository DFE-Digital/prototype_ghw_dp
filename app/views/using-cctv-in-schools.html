Using CCTV in schools

Schools need to carefully consider the necessity, purpose, and privacy implications before installing CCTV systems.

Many schools already use CCTV to enhance the safety and security of sites and to protect pupils, staff and visitors. The use of CCTV in schools must comply with data protection law because it captures images of people, which counts as personal data.

Your use of CCTV must comply with data protection legislation and other relevant legal requirements including:

  • the Freedom of Information Act 2000
  • the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012
  • the Human Rights Act 1998
  • the Surveillance Camera Code of Practice

Before installing CCTV

You must clearly define the purpose for its use and assess whether CCTV is the most effective option for your school’s needs and must explore less intrusive alternatives. You should only install cameras where there is a clear need, ensuring their use is justified, proportionate and respects people’s privacy.

You should think carefully about the types of technology your CCTV system will use. Avoid CCTV with live facial recognition technology. This is because it uses sensitive biometric data which poses a high risk to the rights and freedoms of pupils, staff and visitors. You would need a very strong justification for using it, which is unlikely to apply in most schools.

You should also consider completing a Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before installing CCTV to safeguard the privacy of pupils, staff and visitors. Consult your school’s Data Protection Officer before deciding where to place cameras. They can help ensure your plans meet your data protection and safeguarding responsibilities.

If you decide to install CCTV you must:

  • consult with pupils, parents and staff
  • explain where the cameras will be and what the footage will be used for

Transparency

You must be open and honest about your use of CCTV.

This means:

  • placing signs where CCTV is in use
  • updating your privacy notices, telling staff what you are using CCTV for and; how long footage will be kept and who it may be shared with

You must ensure there are signs in place and clearly visible to inform people that CCTV is in use and explain its purpose.

Record keeping

You must have clear processes for reviewing and managing CCTV footage. Only staff who are trained and authorised should be able to access it, and it must be deleted securely in line with your retention policy

Your subject access request policy should cover CCTV, as people can ask to see footage you have recorded of them. You must respond to requests within one calendar month of receiving the request.

The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) has further guidance on data protection requirements for CCTV.
